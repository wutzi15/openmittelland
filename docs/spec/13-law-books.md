# 13 Law books, courts, credit and debt (Die Gesetzesbücher)

Handbook H p.53-55. Partly in-world chronicle (law text of 1433); the modern-state paragraphs (H p.54-55) describe game rules.

## Law book structure
- Three law books of ten laws each (30 laws). H p.54; chapter 5 says "about 30". H p.18
- Laws can be changed by office holders: finance minister - money laws; justice minister - criminal laws; archbishop - church laws (enact or strike). H p.54
- NPCs with the privilege may change the law books during the world turn. H p.30

## Original 1433 law text (in-world; current laws may differ)
The chronicle says these laws were later extended and modified. H p.54. Stated contents, by book:

Money matters (H p.53):
- Borrowing from moneylenders is allowed.
- Making others compliant with money (bribery) is punishable.
- Paying workers starvation wages is shameful (offence).
- Everyone pays one seventh of their earnings as tax.

Legal matters (H p.53):
- Spying on others or harming others, by whatever means, leads to court.
- Fishing in the princely ponds is strictly forbidden.
- Besieging cities without approval of emperor or prince: banishment, outlaw within Mittelland.
- Robbery (Räubertum): punished most severely. H p.54

Faith matters (H p.54):
- The prince serves the Roman Church; so shall his people.
- Indulgences are pleasing (they cleanse souls).
- Keeping a mistress (Mätressentum) is reprehensible.
- Feasts with wine and song are allowed.
- Church tax: one tenth of everyone's income.

## Courts
- Normal verdicts by city judges on the evidence. If the accused holds a county office, the justice adviser judges; a principality office, the justice minister. H p.54
- Assessors who also decide: usually judges from cities neighbouring the place of accusation, in serious cases the county justice advisers. H p.54
- Evidence must come from spies. H p.18

## Credit, seizure, jury court
- Credit standing depends on: loans, seizures, jury court record, debt tower history, titles, possessions, behaviour toward other office holders, goodwill toward the people. H p.54
- Rich patricians lend at high interest. Loans can be taken voluntarily; at high debt the player may be forced to take loans to cover running costs. H p.54
- If debt exceeds a certain level, creditors may seize part of the goods, transports and storage space; in severe cases whole branches. Creditors have so far always followed such seizures with a suit before a jury court. H p.54-55
- Heavily indebted traders have been thrown into the dungeon without trial by creditors. H p.55
- Jury courts consist of ordinary citizens of a city and are independent of the standing courts. H p.55
- Loan expiry without payment -> lender's bailiffs -> jury court; can be escaped only by paying the jury court a suitable tribute. H p.19
- Debt tower: one year, skip one round (penalty list, H p.34).

### Jury court examples (narrative)
- Citizen Andreas (Kronsbach): seizure of three carts, unpaid loan of 230 gold; acquitted out of pity. H p.55
- Freiherr Hugo: 3,000 gold invested in witnesses; seizure of 14 carts, 3 ships and two complete branches; found guilty by narrow majority; one year in the debt tower; his branch managers continued trading meanwhile. H p.55
