# 14 Family and dynasty (Die Familie)

Handbook H p.56-57 (incl. "Jolandas Tagebuch"); readme 5.9, R p.9.

## Lifespan and health
- The character ages; life expectancy is limited. Handbook: assume you start in 1600 at age 20. H p.56
- Ageing symptoms reduce health over time; illnesses can shorten life further; duels have consequences. H p.56
- A quack or physician (Quacksalber, Medicus) may visit and extend life. H p.56
- Pillory harms health and life expectancy. H p.34
- Tips option warns when life nears its end without children. H p.8

## Marriage
1. Go to a matchmaker (Kupplerin): church exterior, bell tower ("Hochzeitsglocken"). H p.20, R p.9
2. The matchmaker makes an offer and shows a picture of the proposed partner. Who is proposed depends mainly on the player's wealth, office, and standing with the people. H p.56
3. If the player courts the person, generous gifts in the following years are needed to win affection. H p.56, R p.9
4. Wedding location matters: canons may forbid weddings without giving reasons. H p.57. The wedding always takes place in the city where the courted person lives; a small "donation" (bribe) to that city's canon can prevent a veto; a human canon is a risk. R p.9
5. After marriage, standing with the friends of the bride/groom rises. A feast before the wedding night is advisable. H p.57

## Children, will, inheritance
- Children may follow; they can be baptised in church. H p.57
- With age, make a will and name an heir who receives most of the possessions; other children receive a compulsory share. H p.57
- The will is made via "the corresponding privilege" and can be changed any number of times as more children are born. R p.9
- Readme rhetorical hint, not a stated rule: `[EXE text]` ("You don't want the archdiocese to inherit everything, do you?"). What happens without a will is not stated. R p.9
- Disinherited relatives may oppose the main heir for many years. H p.57
- On death: burial in the cemetery; relatives carve an epitaph summarising the life. H p.57
- Play continues across generations with a child after the main character dies. H p.57, R p.9

## Mistress / lover
- Keeping a mistress or lover alongside the spouse is common; it raises standing with like-minded people. H p.61
- It is an offence (1433 law: "Mätressentum ist verwerflich"; evidence example H p.51). H p.54
