## Appendix I - Who appoints whom (Wer ernennt wen?)
H p.35; readme points here, R p.7.

### Principality (Im Fürstentum Mittelland)
| Secular office | Appointed by | Ecclesiastical office | Appointed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regent | drawing lots (Losentscheid) | Archbishop (Erzbischof) | the 3 county bishops |
| Grand chancellor (Großkanzler) | Regent | Inquisitor, Archdeacon (Erzdiakon) | Archbishop |
| Finance minister, Justice minister, Marshal (Marschall) | Grand chancellor | | |
| 3 court councillors (Hofräte) | Finance minister, Justice minister, Marshal | | |

### Each of the three counties (In den drei Grafschaften)
| Secular office | Appointed by | Ecclesiastical office | Appointed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reeve (Vogt) | drawing lots | Bishop (Bischof) | Archbishop |
| Chancellor (Kanzler) | Vogt | Abbot (Abt) | Sacristan, Cellarer, Bishop |
| Finance adviser, Justice adviser, Captain (Hauptmann) | Kanzler | Deacon (Diakon) | Bishop |
| 3 privy councillors (Geheimräte) | Finance adviser, Justice adviser, Captain | Sacristan, Cellarer (Kellermeister) | Abbot |

### Each of the ten cities (In den zehn Städten)
| Secular office | Appointed by | Ecclesiastical office | Appointed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mayor (Bürgermeister) | 3 aldermen | Canon (Domherr) | Bishop of the county the city belongs to |
| Treasurer, Judge, Master builder (Kämmerer, Richter, Baumeister) | Mayor | | |
| 3 aldermen (Ratsherren) | Treasurer, Judge, Master builder | 2 priests (Priester) | Canon |

Derived office counts (from the table): principality 1+1+3+3 = 8 secular, 1+2 = 3 ecclesiastical; per county 1+1+3+3 = 8 secular, 1+1+1+2 = 5 ecclesiastical; per city 1+3+3 = 7 secular, 1+2 = 3 ecclesiastical.

Promotion chain example in the handbook text: mayor -> privy councillor, judge -> mayor, alderman -> judge. H p.17

Readme example contradicts the grouping: it says receiving certificate II lets you produce cloth (Tuch), which the table lists under III. R p.8

Means of production nouns named in the text: Korn - fields (Felder); Fisch - boats (Boote); Bier - kettles (Kessel). H p.12-13. Other goods (from the relations chapter, H p.43-46): wool - sheep; wood - a leased patch of forest; beef - cattle (Rind); ore - mine; wine - wine barrel (Weinfass); fur - tanning vats (Bottiche); glass - glass furnace; salt - mine; cloth - loom (Webstuhl); silver - silver mine; pepper - expedition; weapons - forge (Schmiede).

5. Multi-appointer offices: majority vote? Ties?
6. Is the archdeacon/inquisitor/finance minister etc. one per principality (one each)?
