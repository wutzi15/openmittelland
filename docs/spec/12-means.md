# 12 Means and ways (Mittel und Wege)

Handbook H p.49-52: in-world letters between Landgraf Ullrich (abbot, later bishop of Bergenhöh) and Inquisitor Valeran (spelled "Baleran" once in the subtitle, H p.49). Narrative; rules below are those the letters state.

## Stated rules
- Player activities over time: trade, pay taxes, send transports, set wages, donate to the people, buy indulgences, rise in offices and gain privileges. H p.49
- Bishop of a county is appointed by the archbishop. H p.49; consistent with H p.35.
- Archbishop is appointed by the bishops of the three counties. H p.52; consistent with H p.35.
- Deacon privilege: preferred consideration in the bishop election. H p.50
- Narrative: Archbishop Gottfried "is not well disposed toward secular office holders", so a secular candidate is judged to have little chance with him. Whether appointers have such a bias as a mechanic is not stated. H p.50
- Different faith makes bribery require an "astronomically" high sum to have an effect. H p.50; consistent with H p.7.
- Spies can be hired against high office holders; they charge per year. H p.50
- Strategy recipe stated: provoke a rival, watch him, sabotage him so he sues; buy an indulgence first so you stand innocent; the rival spends on witnesses; then denounce him to his appointer, who may dismiss him. H p.51
- Narrative case: the innocent defendant was acquitted despite large witness and court spending by the accuser, and the accuser had to bear the trial costs. H p.51

- Bribing judges and the justice adviser in the year before a trial improves their favour. H p.51
- Denouncing a deacon to his bishop after an unfounded lawsuit led to dismissal. H p.51
- Evidence types collected on the archbishop: keeping a mistress, bribery, running a robber camp (all offences). H p.51
- Blackmail can force an appointer to vote for the blackmailer. H p.51-52
- Holding an office whose holder is targeted carries risk for the player too. H p.52
