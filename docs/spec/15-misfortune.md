# 15 Strokes of fate (Schicksalsschläge)

Handbook H p.58-59: in-world farewell letter of master builder Thomas (1623). Narrative; rules below are those the letter states or illustrates.

## Narrative content (illustrations from the letter; not confirmed as mechanics unless cross-referenced)
- Fate can be positive or negative. Some priests claim lighting candles or using holy water in a church or cathedral wins God's favour (hint that church actions affect fate). H p.58
- Insurance against disasters is mentioned only in this narrative ("keinerlei Versicherung ... abgeschlossen"; "eine Versicherung abgeschlossen"); no insurance menu is described in chapter 5. H p.58-59
- Flood (Jahrhundertflut): fields flooded, grain harvest destroyed, boats lost at sea. H p.58
- Siege of a city: carts had to turn back; very low sale price (4 gold per bushel in the example). H p.58
- Thomas bought storage and rented it out profitably after the disaster ("deren Vermietung ... gute Gewinne"). H p.58
- Holding an office brings an income (master builder: "erkleckliches Einkommen"). H p.58
- Fire in storage destroyed more than half of it. H p.58
- Sabotage of competitors' storage by hired men ("in Brand stecken") to raise prices. H p.59
- A rival office holder (canon) had him spied on and sued him; Thomas suspects the witnesses were bribed; eight witnesses testified to crimes he says he never committed. H p.59

- Three judges decided. H p.59
- Penalty: a fine of 780 gold each year for four years. H p.59
- The mayor dismissed him from office after the conviction. H p.59
- Back-tax payment demanded (630 gold). H p.59
- Creditors seized everything and he went to the dungeon for good. H p.59
- Prudent traders protect themselves with security, insurance, or smaller trade volume. H p.59
